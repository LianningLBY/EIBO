We submit “Finite-sample certificates for Bayes accuracy gains from feature fusion under block dependence: An evaluation on QUIC traffic” for consideration in Information Fusion. We develop lower confidence bounds on the improvement in Bayes accuracy from adding a feature to an existing representation. The method allows arbitrary dependence within each time block and assumes conditional independence across blocks.

We compare four bounds on the same held-out flows and fitted rules in two encrypted traffic corpora. For the 44-class CESNET-QUICEXT-25 XS cohort, the pooled daily bounds on the gain from adding packet timing are 2.035 and 1.935 percentage points in two disjoint evaluation windows. After omitting the leading coarse cell and accounting for selection among omissions, the daily bounds for the remaining cells are 0.405 and 0.298 points. We report sensitivity to calendar alignment and to a model that permits dependence between the even- and odd-date groups.

The study fits Information Fusion’s scope in feature fusion and network security. The method decomposes the gain into nonnegative contributions that can be bounded simultaneously. A controlled simulation shows how the concentration of gain and the number of occupied cells affect the choice between pooling and separate cell bounds. The result helps readers assess whether a new observation is informative enough to establish a gain under a stated sampling model.
