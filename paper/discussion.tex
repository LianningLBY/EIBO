\subsection{Robustness across corpora}
\label{sec:discussion/robustness}

The primary result is one corpus's number, so we repeated the measurement on an
independent one. On CESNET-QUIC22, 3,321 pairs yield an upper-side fraction of
$0.1608$ ($534/3321$), a lower-side fraction of $0.9967$ ($3310/3321$), and a
joint gate of $0.1575$ ($523/3321$) (\Cref{fig:cross_corpus}a). The lower side
reproduces across the two corpora, $0.9967$ against $0.9960$, which is not a
foregone conclusion: it is computed by a different method from the upper side and
on different data.

The upper side does not reproduce, and we state the two facts that govern how it
should be read. First, the joint gate combines a full-$n$ upper side with a
lower side capped at 20,000 flows per service, and the cap binds on 2,145 of the
3,321 pairs. Because a lower bound computed from less data is weaker, the cap
can only shrink $\epslo$ and therefore can only understate the gate; the
reported figure is a floor, and the bias runs in the conservative direction.
Second, the comparison depends on how much evidence per pair is required, and
the dependence is not monotone. On the $\epshi < 0.5$ stratum the gap is $-18.3$
percentage points unmatched, $-15.5$ at a $13{,}049$-flow floor, $-9.3$ at
$20{,}000$, $-13.0$ at $40{,}000$, and $-15.4$ at $80{,}000$
(\Cref{fig:cross_corpus}b). We quote the stratum values rather than the figure's,
which plots the joint-gate fraction over a slightly different subset; the two
series share the same minimum and shape but are not the same quantity. The series
minimises in the middle and widens again, so ``matched evidence'' is not one
well-defined point and the minimum is not quoted as the matched value.

Whether the gap survives at matched evidence turns on the dependence structure.
The 990 pairs form a complete graph over 45 services ($\binom{45}{2}$), as do the
$3{,}321$ replication pairs over 82 services, so pairs sharing a service are
correlated and a pair-level bootstrap is anti-conservative. Under a
service-cluster bootstrap, non-reproduction remains significant unmatched
($-18.3$ points, interval $[-27.4,-10.0]$) and at the $13{,}049$-flow floor
($-15.5$, $[-28.4,-2.9]$), but the intervals at the $20{,}000$, $40{,}000$, and
$80{,}000$ floors are $[-24.2,+5.2]$, $[-30.1,+4.3]$, and $[-33.6,+2.9]$, all of
which include zero. The residual gap at deep matching is therefore not
statistically resolved at this sample size: it is negative at every stratum as a
point estimate, and the scale-matched defence of it does not survive a
defensible interval. The correction is itself informative, in that it widens the
unmatched gap's interval by a factor of $2.77$ while moving the lower side's by
only $1.22\times$; the lower side is saturated, so it reproduces across corpora
under either construction, and only the gap claim is affected.

\subsection{Channel sensitivity}
\label{sec:discussion/sensitivity}

The declared channel excludes the host identifier, and we measured what
including it would change rather than asserting that the exclusion is
immaterial. Two arms are compared at matched declared alphabet sizes: one that
appends a hash of the destination address to the prefix symbol, and one that
appends a hash of a random per-flow identifier. Both use the same flows and the
same splits. At matched resolution the two arms separate before either
saturates, and the matched difference in the radius-free estimate grows with the
identifier cardinality, from $0.0271$ at $B{=}2$ through $0.0885$ and $0.2271$ to
$0.3136$ at $B{=}256$ on the test split, then falls to $0.0150$ at $B{=}65{,}536$
where both arms saturate and the comparison loses its content
(\Cref{fig:sensitivity}). The cardinality-only arm does not produce a comparable
separation, so the effect tracks identifier content and not merely alphabet size.

The scope of this result is narrow and we keep it that way. The identifier
effect is established at the level of the radius-free estimate $\widehat{tv}$,
because the certificate-level comparison is unavailable at every rung: one arm
saturates, so $\epshi$ cannot be compared between them, and no two-sided
interval is emitted by this experiment at all. The citable statement is that
identifier content changes radius-free separation beyond a pure cardinality
effect. It is not that the identifier destroys the certificate or degrades the
bracket, and we make no claim of that kind.

A related check tells us something about the address field itself. The
destination address is not a function of the server name: 8,323 distinct server
names map to more than one destination address in the sampled rows. Address
structure and name structure are therefore different quantities, and an
evaluation that randomizes one is not automatically protected against the other.

\subsection{The unknown-class mass at a deployment site}
\label{sec:discussion/missing_mass}

The bracket is defined over the services present in a corpus, so it says nothing
about services that are absent from it. We quantify that gap as a
deployment-site quantity and not as a universal prior. Over roughly five million
flows with 22,108 distinct identifiers, the estimated missing-service mass is
$0.00202$, with a nonparametric lower bound of about 18,150 unseen identifiers.
The estimate grows with subsampling, by roughly $3.7\times$ at a 1:10 rate and
$11.7\times$ at 1:100, which is the direction expected for a well-sampled
distribution but also a reminder that the number belongs to the sampling rate at
which it was measured. We report it as a site-specific additive term, and we do
not fold it into the bracket.

\subsection{Two certified objects that must not be ranked}
\label{sec:discussion/objects}

Two certified objects coexist: the $k$-prefix symbol law carrying the headline
bracket, and a whole-flow rectified object on the same flows. Their informative
fractions are numerically close, and presenting that as a cross-check would be
wrong. They agree only where each uses its own radius convention, and holding the
convention common separates them by hundreds of pairs: the statistics differ by
about $2.7\times$ and the radius constants by about $7.3\times$ in opposite
directions, so the near-equality is cancellation rather than corroboration. We
report both without ranking them.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion/limitations}

The certificate covers 34.1\% of service pairs on the primary corpus. The
remaining pairs are not indistinguishable; for most of them no certificate
exists at these $k$ and $n$, which \Cref{sec:results/horizon} establishes as a
consequence of the radius rather than of the traffic. That should be read as an
absence of certification, not as evidence of closeness.

Every number here is measured on a subset whose occupancy pass is provisional
pending full-corpus confirmation, and on a single protocol. Both sides of the
bracket carry a confidence level, and the lower side's is conditional on the
fitted decision function, so the interval is only as strong as its weaker
endpoint when read as a two-sided statement. The growth question that motivated
the estimator study remains open, and we present no estimate of the growth rate
anywhere in this paper. The large-$k$ divisibility bound is conditional on a
realizability assumption: it is available where the plug-in certificate is lost,
which makes it useful, but it is not distribution-free and that limits how
strongly it may be worded.

\subsection{Ethics considerations}
\label{sec:discussion/ethics}

All measurements use a public corpus collected by its operators under their own
data-handling framework. We consume flow-level metadata only, attempt no
deanonymization, and report no per-flow identifier values. The result is a
defensive auditing instrument; the appendix sets out the full considerations.
