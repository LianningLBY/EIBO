\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We turned a detector score into a statement about what no detector can achieve on
a given window, by measuring a two-sided bracket per service pair on real QUIC
traffic. At the coarsest pre-registered channel, 336 of 990 pairs (33.9\%) admit
one. The horizon is short and its failure arithmetical: the radius grows with the
alphabet while the threshold does not, so the upper-side set falls from 340 pairs
at $M{=}1$ to none at $M{=}8$; beyond it, a divisibility bound keeps the large-$k$
regime non-empty. The standard chain-rule estimator saturates under a
known-truth control at fine resolution, so the growth rate it reports cannot
answer the question that motivated the certificate.
