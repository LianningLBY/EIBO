- **NEW (D139): an assumption-free open-set error floor.** The paper states it makes "no multi-class or open-set reduction" and that aggregation "would require an argument we do not give". **That is false, and so was my stronger claim that the reduction is impossible.** With `M = K+1` classes and a uniform prior,
  `E[err] >= S_known / (M(M-1))`, where `S_known = sum_{i<j, i,j known} (1 - tv_ij)`,
  holds with **no assumption on the unknown class**, because the discarded unknown-class terms enter the sum with positive sign — dropping them only weakens the bound. The `1/(2K(K-1))` constant I used yesterday was wrong by exactly a factor of two; `M(M-1)` is correct and reduces to Le Cam at `M=2`. Verified on constructed cases and **1500 randomised configurations including adversarial placements of U, zero violations**. On the corpus (43 certified services + unknown): **floor ≈ 0.118**. Limits recorded: standard Le Cam applied carefully rather than a new mechanism; uniform prior required; loose by construction; not falsifiable from the existing per-pair artifact, which measures a different object. Artifact: `openset_floor_assumption_free.md`.

## Feasibility of the reviewers' named addition — both routes evaluated (D134-D138)

Round 6's clear-accept threshold is "one substantive methodological addition plus the Sec 3.3 coverage calibration", and the report names the addition as a finite-sample lower bound or a pairwise→open-set reduction. Both were evaluated on 2026-09-16. **Neither yields the unconditional addition the reviewers want.**

- **Pairwise→open-set reduction: NOT impossible — my earlier "impossibility" was WRONG and is retracted (D138, superseding D136).** Independent scrutiny found two errors, both of which I reproduced. (a) **The summation step is valid for all K.** Building the binary test from the classifier itself — `psi_ij(X) = 1{phi(X)=i}` — gives, under `Y=j`, `{phi=i} ⊆ {phi≠j}`, so the test's error is at most `err_j`; Le Cam then applies unconditionally, and `E[err] >= (1/(2K(K-1))) sum_{i≠j}(1 - tv_ij)` is a **correct** generalisation. My "more classes ⇒ more information" objection was invalid because the reduction uses the same classifier, hence the same information set. (b) **The "counterexample" was not one**: its true Bayes error is **0.075269** (per-class `[0.014113, 0.0875×5]`) against a floor of **0.045161**, so the formula holds with slack. Separability would require `rest = 0`, i.e. `tv = 1` and a floor of 0 — the two conditions I claimed cannot co-occur.
  - **Root cause (M146):** my verification script scored `argmax(P,axis=1) == arange(K)`, which assumes class `i`'s modal symbol is the `i`-th column — true only for index-aligned one-hot supports. The duplicated-support mode broke it, so I measured a *mislabelled* classifier. With the correct uniform-prior Bayes error: **0 violations in 400 cases** (vs 100 with the broken scorer). The "100 of 400" figure was a harness bug, not evidence.
  - **Also not ours:** the surviving observation ("the floor degenerates in the near-separated regime") is a direct corollary of Le Cam. Le Cam, Fano, Assouad and Tsybakov are **absent from `references.bib`**, so this would have to be *cited* as standard, never claimed.
  - **What IS available and verified:** a **conditional open-set corollary** — `E[err] >= (1/2)(1 - max_i tv(P_i,U))`, equivalently the sum form over i — which I tested in all three variants (mean/max/min) on benign and adversarial configurations and which holds in every case, including U close to one known class. It needs a measurement (distance from U to the known laws) that X13 does not supply, since X13 measures **mass**. The artifact is marked **DO NOT CITE** pending rewrite.
  - **Paper impact: NONE.** `measurement_design.tex:20-33`, `abstract.tex:10` and `introduction.tex:78` already state the per-pair scope correctly and consistently; the headline numbers (336/990, cluster CI, 340 upper / 336 joint) are untouched by all of this. **No paper edit is required, and the planned one is withdrawn.**
- **Finite-sample lower bound: achievable but modest (D134/D135, artifact `finite_sample_lower_bound_feasibility.md`).** The implementation reproduces Michel et al. Appendix B.1 Corollary 4 to 1e-9, confirming the "asymptotic" description. Simulating the correct object (sup over the **full cutoff path**) gives an exact finite-sample quantile ≈ **0.86x** the asymptotic constant, flat in m; the conservative fallback `sqrt(2 log(6/α)) = 3.6275` is only **3–11%** above the correct value at the small counts where it fires. So the gain is small, and the source paper itself proposes the simulation alternative — this is *tightening*, not new construction. Worth doing for rigour (removes the "nominal" caveat and the fallback branch), not for novelty.
